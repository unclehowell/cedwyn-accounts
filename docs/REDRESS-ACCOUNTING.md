# Disclosure redress and accounting controls

Verified legal-rule snapshot: 26 November 2026. Jurisdiction: England and Wales unless the rule specifies otherwise. Re-check each live source, the applicable institutional policy and the law in force at the event date before relying on any entry.

## Purpose and boundary

This is a financial and evidence-management aid for current-year information-access and service-handling matters. It is not a damages tariff, a finding of institutional liability, a legal opinion, or authority to issue an invoice. A delay, refusal, narrow interpretation, failed search, later disclosure or missed response date is an event to investigate; it does not, on its own, create a debt owed to the requester.

## The three hourly rates must not be conflated

| Rate / threshold | Whose time? | What it is | What it is not |
|---|---|---|---|
| £24 per hour (CPR 46.5 / PD46 para 3.4) | A litigant in person | The default assessment rate for qualifying work in litigation where the court orders LiP costs and financial loss is not proved; subject to the two-thirds cap against the represented-party costs figure, reasonableness and the order | A rate for time waiting for FOI/SAR disclosure, writing routine chasers, preparing complaints, or issuing a private pre-action invoice |
| £25 per hour (FOIA Fees Regulations reg 4(4)) | Authority staff / persons performing permitted tasks for the authority | A notional rate for an authority's estimate of specified section 12 compliance costs | Compensation to the requester, a market rate, or an amount the requester can invoice back |
| £50 per hour plus VAT (Glamorgan Archives quote dated 6 Nov 2026, PW-12) | Archive staff | A supplier's quoted price for staff searches, estimated 1–3 hours per reference, including a digitised image of any item found; free searchroom inspection was offered as an alternative | A benchmark for valuing the requester's own time, or a sum recoverable from the archive or another institution |

FOIA section 12 cost limits are £450 for most public authorities and £600 for central government / bodies listed in Part I of Schedule 1. The £25/hour estimate is confined to permitted locating, retrieval and extraction activities. The authority must give the relevant estimate and advice/assistance duties apply. This is separate from the £24 LiP costs rule.

Sources: [CPR 46.5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1998/3132/part/46/crossheading/costs-payable-by-or-to-particular-persons); [PD46](https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part-46-costs-special-cases/practice-direction-46-costs-special-cases); [FOIA Fees Regulations reg 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2004/3244/regulation/4); [ICO section 12 guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/foi/freedom-of-information-and-environmental-information-regulations/section-12-requests-where-the-cost-of-compliance-exceeds-the-appropriate-limit/).

## Financial redress routes

### 1. FOI / EIR statutory access and enforcement

FOIA and EIR provide access and regulatory / tribunal routes. FOI response time is normally 20 working days; EIR normally 20 working days, with a qualifying extension up to 40 for complexity and volume. EIR regulation 11 has a statutory internal-representations process; a FOIA internal review is not itself a statutory requirement (it is expected by the section 45 Code and is normally sought before the regulator). The Information Commission can investigate and require appropriate compliance but does not award requester compensation for an FOI breach. The ICO asks for complaints within six weeks of the final response or last substantive contact; this is its complaint-handling guidance, not a statutory limitation period. Tribunal appeals normally have a 28-day window after the decision notice.

**Accounting treatment:** missed deadlines, refusal notices, late or silent replies, review delays and subsequent disclosure are code 9900 evidence events. They do not generate a claim value automatically. A separate legal cause of action, remedy scheme or evidenced loss must be identified.

Sources: [FOIA s10](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/36/section/10); [FOIA s50](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/36/section/50); [EIR reg 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2004/3391/regulation/5); [EIR reg 7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2004/3391/regulation/7); [EIR reg 11](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2004/3391/regulation/11); [ICO complaint guidance](https://ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint/foi-and-eir-complaints/what-to-expect-from-the-ico-when-making-an-foi-or-eir-complaint/); [GOV.UK Tribunal appeal guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/information-rights-appeal-against-the-commissioners-decision).

### 2. UK GDPR / Data Protection Act compensation

Article 82 UK GDPR and DPA 2018 section 168 allow compensation for material or non-material damage caused by an infringement, including distress. The claimant must establish the infringement, actual damage and causal link. An infringement alone is not enough; the remedy is compensatory, not punitive, and no fixed hourly tariff applies. A subject access request normally has a one-month period, potentially extended by up to two further months in the qualifying circumstances and with proper notice. DPA 2018 Part 3 access for law-enforcement processing is a distinct regime and must not be treated as an ordinary UK GDPR SAR.

Sources: [UK GDPR Article 82](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/82); [DPA 2018 s168](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/section/168); [ICO SAR guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/subject-access-requests/a-guide-to-subject-access/); [DPA 2018 Part 3 s45](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/section/45).

### 3. Ombudsman service redress

The Public Services Ombudsman for Wales can recommend a proportionate remedy where a qualifying body has acted wrongly and caused injustice. Remedies may include apology, explanation, remedial action, direct/indirect financial loss, distress, inconvenience or exceptional time and trouble beyond what is normally expected. It is not a tariff for every hour spent researching. In most cases apology/explanation may be sufficient. The PSOW may decline where a court remedy exists or should reasonably be used. Confirm the body's complaint process, the PSOW's remit and current time limits for each case; the usual one-year awareness window and complaint-process expectations should be treated as urgent controls, not assumed universal rules.

Sources: [PSOW remedies](https://www.ombudsman.wales/fact-sheets/remedies/); [PSOW financial redress](https://www.ombudsman.wales/fact-sheets/financial-redress/); [PSOW procedure](https://www.ombudsman.wales/fact-sheets/complaints-against-public-bodies-our-procedure/).

### 4. Water-company Guaranteed Standards Scheme (Wales)

Ofwat's Wales table lists £20 for a written complaint not actioned on time and a £10 late-payment penalty. Citizens Advice describes the usual standard as a response within 10 working days, with the £20 payment then due within the scheme period and a further £10 potentially available if the payment is late and requested within three months. Exceptions may apply. It is a service-standard remedy, not a general FOI/SAR compensation amount.

The £20 Dŵr Cymru cheque in the workbench is deliberately classified as **unallocated / suspense**. Match it to the cheque/remittance letter, underlying complaint subject, receipt date, response date, applicable GSS standard, any exception, payment date and bank clearance before moving it to GSS income. If it was for a qualifying water/sewerage written complaint, code 4100 may be appropriate; do not assume that from the amount alone.

Sources: [Ofwat standards of service](https://www.ofwat.gov.uk/households/supply-and-standards/standards-of-service/); [Citizens Advice Wales](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/wales/consumer/water/complaints-about-your-water-supply/complaining-about-your-water-company/); [CCW](https://www.ccw.org.uk/faq/what-standards-are-guaranteed-by-water-and-sewerage-companies/).

### 5. Court costs and small claims

CPR 46.5 is a rule for costs within litigation where the court makes a costs order. A LiP's reasonable time may be allowed at the prescribed rate where financial loss is not proved, and the two-thirds cap applies. It is not a freestanding cause of action and does not permit a requester to invoice an institution for FOI or SAR administration. The small-claims track is a procedural allocation for qualifying claims (usually up to £10,000, subject to exceptions); it does not create liability. Costs are tightly limited on the small-claims track. Only sue where an enforceable debt or properly established cause of action exists, with proof of defendant, duty, breach, causation, loss, limitation and pre-action compliance.

Sources: [CPR Part 27](https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part27); [CPR 46.5 / PD46](https://www.justice.gov.uk/courts/procedure-rules/civil/rules/part-46-costs-special-cases/practice-direction-46-costs-special-cases).

### 6. Other civil claims and exceptional public-law torts

Contract, negligence, breach of confidence / misuse of private information, statutory causes of action, misfeasance in public office and Human Rights Act claims each require their own legal ingredients. Do not select one solely because an institution's response was poor. Misfeasance in public office is particularly demanding: it requires proof of a public officer's bad-faith abuse of power and the requisite knowledge or recklessness as to illegality and probable injury, with causation and loss. A Human Rights Act claim requires an identified Convention right, victim status and an unlawful act by a public authority; HRA proceedings normally have a one-year period, subject to the court's equitable discretion. Section 77 FOIA is a specific criminal offence about record interference with intent to prevent disclosure; it does not create a private fine or damages claim for the requester.

Sources: [HRA 1998 s7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/42/section/7); [FOIA s77](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/36/section/77); [Three Rivers DC v Bank of England [2001] UKHL 16](https://www.bailii.org/uk/cases/UKHL/2001/16.html).

## Limitation and urgent deadlines

Never use one generic deadline field as the limitation period for every route. The system holds (a) the source request/review/tribunal deadline, and (b) a separately entered **verified limitation date** for the potential civil cause of action. Treat the ICO six-week complaint window, EIR regulation 11's 40-working-day representation window, tribunal's 28-day appeal window, PSOW complaint timeframe, HRA one-year period, judicial review's promptness requirement and civil limitation periods as separate clocks.

Many ordinary contract and tort claims have a six-year limitation period, but this is not universal. Limitation Act 1980 section 32 may postpone time for fraud, deliberate concealment or mistake in certain claims until discovery or when discovery could reasonably have occurred; there are exceptions and property-related limits. It does not automatically revive a historic claim or remove a longstop. Obtain cause-specific legal advice before relying on it. Judicial review must be brought promptly and is subject to a short longstop; record that separately.

Sources: [Limitation Act 1980 s2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1980/58/section/2); [Limitation Act 1980 s32](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1980/58/section/32); [HRA s7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1998/42/section/7).

## Evidence and anti-double-counting rules

- Link each line to the exact source record, request/complaint reference, response, event date and relevant evidence catalogue IDs.
- Distinguish a no-reply snapshot, late response, substantive refusal, partial disclosure, later discovery of earlier records, and a quiet disclosure made in another thread. Check WhatDoTheyKnow and email across threads before describing silence or concealment.
- Record the actual time spent only as an evidential fact. Do not multiply by £24, £25 or an archive supplier's hourly quote unless a separate legal basis permits that exact calculation.
- One request with eight chase emails remains one request event and its related activity; do not turn each chaser into an independent charge.
- Keep search/copy quotes separate from paid expenses. Never claim a quoted search fee as a loss unless it was actually incurred and is legally recoverable.
- Record a proposed, disputed, accepted, awarded and paid amount as separate states. A proposed sum is not a debtor balance.
- Do not label an institution's actions as fraud, concealment, deliberate suppression or government involvement solely because records emerge later or a response was delayed. Keep the primary record and the institution's explanation side by side.
- The current-year workbench is separate from the held 1987/1989 historic redress memorandum. A new disclosure event may be a research lead; it does not itself meet the reopening threshold or alter that gate. There is no general rule allowing a private citizen to charge half a solicitor's hourly rate.

## Storage, privacy and publication

The workbench holds user-entered events and financial schedules in browser localStorage only. It is not encrypted, not synced and not transmitted by the workbench, but local browser data is not a secure vault: use a trusted device/profile and export a JSON backup. Only the generic rules catalogue and JavaScript are published; personal claim data is not generated into the static public ledger. Never enter private bank data, identity credentials or sensitive personal information in public artefacts.
